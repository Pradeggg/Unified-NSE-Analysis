# Deep Research: Six Q1 FY27 Result Candidates

**Companies:** BSE, Coforge, Elgi Equipments, India Glycols, Bharat Electronics (BEL), Syrma SGS Technology  
**Research date:** 16 August 2026  
**Price reference:** 14 August 2026 close, as displayed by Screener on the research date  
**Financial basis:** Consolidated results unless stated otherwise; ₹ crore except per-share data  

## Executive conclusion

The best businesses in the set are **BSE, BEL and Coforge**. The best balance between operating momentum and valuation is **India Glycols**, but it has lower business quality and greater cyclicality. **Elgi Equipments** is a high-quality global industrial compounder whose current valuation leaves limited near-term margin of safety. **Syrma SGS** reported the strongest headline quarter, but its valuation already discounts a long runway of near-perfect execution.

**Risk-adjusted ranking at current prices:** India Glycols, Coforge, Elgi Equipments, BEL, BSE, Syrma SGS.  
**Business-quality ranking irrespective of price:** BSE, BEL, Coforge, Elgi Equipments, Syrma SGS, India Glycols.

This ranking is not a buy/sell recommendation. It deliberately penalizes demanding valuations and earnings that depend on acquisitions, cycles, or working-capital intensity.

## 1. BSE Limited

### Investment case

BSE is no longer merely a legacy exchange. Its economic engine combines transaction fees, market data, listings, mutual-fund distribution and other capital-market infrastructure. Exchange liquidity is reflexive: deeper liquidity attracts participants, which creates more liquidity. That produces a rare network-effect business with very high incremental margins.

Q1 FY27 was exceptional: revenue rose to ₹1,566 crore from ₹958 crore, operating profit to ₹1,046 crore from ₹625 crore, and PAT to ₹873 crore from ₹538 crore. OPM expanded to 67% from 65%. FY24–FY26 revenue increased from ₹1,568 crore to ₹5,124 crore and PAT from ₹772 crore to ₹2,487 crore. The company is debt-free and its recent ROCE is about 60%.

### What the market may be pricing in

At roughly 49.6x trailing earnings, the market assumes that BSE sustains meaningful derivatives activity and high incremental margins. A material regulatory change to product structures, fees, expiry schedules or transaction economics can reset that assumption quickly. Earnings are also tied to capital-market participation and volumes, so current profitability should not be treated as bond-like.

### Verdict and checkpoints

**Verdict:** Best franchise in the basket, but current price offers weak downside protection. Prefer watching for valuation compression or evidence that earnings power has structurally stepped up beyond current assumptions.

Monitor derivatives market share, active client/liquidity metrics, regulatory circulars, data/listing revenue mix, and whether OPM remains above 60% without relying on unusually buoyant volumes.

## 2. Coforge

### Investment case

Coforge has built a differentiated technology-services franchise around financial services, travel/transport and insurance, while using acquisitions to broaden capability and scale. Q1 FY27 revenue was ₹5,528 crore versus ₹3,704 crore, operating profit ₹1,058 crore versus ₹577 crore, and reported PAT ₹532 crore versus ₹356 crore. OPM improved to 19% from 16%.

FY24–FY26 revenue rose from ₹9,009 crore to ₹16,403 crore, while PAT increased from ₹836 crore to ₹1,745 crore. FY26 operating cash flow of about ₹1,792 crore exceeded reported PAT and free cash flow was about ₹1,197 crore. The balance sheet remains comfortable, although acquisition accounting has increased interest and depreciation.

### Critical normalization

Management's official release highlighted 49% revenue growth and 110% PAT growth, while the consolidated Screener series shows reported PAT growth of about 49%. The difference likely reflects the company's chosen adjusted/attributable comparison and acquisition-related items. Encora also makes a simple YoY comparison non-organic. The investment case should therefore be judged on organic constant-currency growth, integration synergies, margin retention and cash conversion—not the headline PAT percentage alone.

### Verdict and checkpoints

**Verdict:** Most attractive quality-growth candidate in the set if Encora integration and organic growth hold. The valuation is not cheap, but it is less stretched than BSE, BEL or Syrma relative to visible earnings momentum.

Monitor organic constant-currency growth, large-deal wins and executable order book, top-client concentration, attrition, utilization, EBIT margin, acquisition debt, and CFO/PAT. Also investigate the substantial fall in reported FII ownership rather than assuming it is a business signal.

## 3. Elgi Equipments

### Investment case

Elgi is an Indian compressor manufacturer with a meaningful global footprint and aftermarket potential. Its installed base creates recurring service and spares revenue, while India, North America and a possible European turnaround provide multiple growth levers. Management's strategic ambition is to become the world's third-largest compressor company by 2035–36.

Q1 FY27 revenue rose 22.5% to ₹1,062 crore, operating profit 28.1% to ₹155 crore and PAT 19.8% to ₹103 crore. OPM improved to 15% from 14%. FY24–FY26 revenue increased from ₹3,218 crore to ₹3,951 crore and PAT from ₹312 crore to ₹430 crore. FY26 CFO was about ₹454 crore and free cash flow about ₹348 crore. Borrowings of roughly ₹533 crore against equity plus reserves of about ₹2,232 crore are manageable.

### Quality caveats

Inventory and cash-conversion cycles remain structurally important in a global industrial business; reported inventory days around 134 and cash-conversion cycle around 114 days deserve attention. The quarter also included restructuring costs, while a separately disclosed tariff refund is non-recurring. Global industrial demand, input costs, currency, tariffs and Chinese price competition can all affect margins.

### Verdict and checkpoints

**Verdict:** High-quality long-duration industrial story, but the price assumes a successful global scaling journey. It is more compelling on a pullback or after sustained evidence of European improvement and aftermarket mix expansion.

Monitor geography-level growth and profitability, aftermarket contribution, Europe breakeven trajectory, North American momentum, inventory/receivables, ROCE, and normalized margin excluding restructuring/refund items.

## 4. India Glycols

### Investment case

India Glycols operates across bio-based and performance chemicals, potable alcohol/spirits and nutraceutical/natural products. Its renewable feedstock positioning differentiates it from conventional petrochemical producers, but the economics remain exposed to input prices, commodity spreads, regulation and business mix.

Q1 FY27 revenue increased 8.7% to ₹1,130 crore, operating profit 13.3% to ₹170 crore and PAT 32.9% to ₹97 crore. OPM rose to 15% from 14%. The most important bridge is finance cost, which declined to ₹25 crore from ₹45 crore; consequently, PAT growth materially exceeded operating growth. FY24–FY26 revenue increased from ₹3,294 crore to ₹4,211 crore and PAT from ₹173 crore to ₹293 crore.

### Corporate-structure issue

The proposed/reported separation involving the spirits and nutraceutical businesses can improve strategic clarity and valuation discovery, but it adds execution, allocation and comparability risk. Investors must trace exactly which assets, debt, cash flows and minority interests sit in each entity after implementation. Until that reconciliation is complete, a simplistic sum-of-the-parts premium is unsafe.

### Verdict and checkpoints

**Verdict:** Lowest headline valuation and therefore the most interesting value candidate, but not the highest-quality company. The rerating case requires sustainable debt reduction, improved ROCE and clearer segment economics—not merely lower interest expense for one quarter.

Monitor net debt and interest coverage, segment EBITDA, chemical spreads/feedstock prices, ethanol and liquor regulation, related-party/corporate restructuring terms, ROCE, and CFO relative to PAT.

## 5. Bharat Electronics

### Investment case

BEL is a strategic defense-electronics company with entrenched customer relationships, qualified products, technical barriers and alignment with India's defense indigenization. Its balance sheet is nearly debt-free, and long-cycle defense programs provide visibility that most industrial businesses lack.

Q1 FY27 revenue rose 24.9% to ₹5,547 crore, but operating profit increased only 12.1% to ₹1,388 crore and PAT 8.9% to ₹1,055 crore. OPM declined to 25% from 28%. The official company release similarly reported PBT of ₹1,402.83 crore, up 8.81%. FY24–FY26 revenue increased from ₹20,268 crore to ₹27,610 crore and PAT from ₹3,985 crore to ₹6,062 crore.

### Cash-flow and valuation caveat

FY26 CFO of about ₹1,541 crore was far below PAT, reflecting working-capital absorption; debtor days near 170 underline the government/customer collection cycle. That is not automatically a credit problem, but it makes order-book growth less valuable if cash conversion deteriorates. At roughly 48.9x earnings, the market already recognizes BEL's moat and defense tailwind.

Monitor order inflow and order-book execution, gross/EBITDA margin, advances and receivables, CFO/PAT, non-defense revenue, exports, R&D intensity, and government procurement/payment timing.

## 6. Syrma SGS Technology

### Investment case

Syrma participates in India's electronics manufacturing services opportunity across industrial, automotive, consumer, healthcare and other verticals. Scale, design capability, customer qualification and supply-chain execution can gradually improve competitive positioning, while domestic manufacturing incentives and China+1 diversification provide structural demand.

Q1 FY27 was the strongest reported quarter in this group: revenue rose 68.3% to ₹1,589 crore, operating profit 86.2% to ₹162 crore and PAT 112% to ₹106 crore. OPM improved to 10% from 9%. FY24–FY26 revenue increased from ₹3,154 crore to ₹4,819 crore and PAT from ₹124 crore to ₹346 crore. Borrowings declined from about ₹665 crore in FY25 to ₹400 crore in FY26.

### Why the stock remains high risk

EMS is typically a thin-margin, working-capital-intensive business. At about 76x trailing earnings, even excellent growth may be insufficient if margin, customer mix or utilization disappoints. Promoter ownership has declined over recent years, and investors should distinguish legitimate capital raising/employee dilution from economically concerning selling. Revenue concentration and the split between pass-through material value and value-added manufacturing are critical.

### Verdict and checkpoints

**Verdict:** Best earnings momentum, worst valuation asymmetry. The company must sustain very high growth, improve capital efficiency and protect margins simply to justify the current price.

Monitor order book and executable schedule, top-five customer concentration, vertical mix, OPM, ROCE, working-capital days, capacity utilization, capex returns, dilution/promoter transactions, and cash conversion.

## Portfolio interpretation

- **For quality at a reasonable growth-adjusted price:** Coforge is the clearest candidate, subject to acquisition normalization.
- **For value/re-rating:** India Glycols offers the widest possible rerating, but also the lowest earnings predictability.
- **For long-duration watchlists:** BSE, BEL and Elgi are excellent businesses where entry price matters greatly.
- **For aggressive momentum investors only:** Syrma's operating trend is powerful, but the bear-case loss is the largest in the group.

No stock has an unambiguous margin of safety under the base cases. A disciplined investor should demand either a lower entry price or new evidence that raises normalized EPS without relying on one-offs.
