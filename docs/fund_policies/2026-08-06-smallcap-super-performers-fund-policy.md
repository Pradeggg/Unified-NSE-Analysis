## 1. Executive Summary

The Smallcap Super Performers Portfolio is an internal research and paper-trading model portfolio designed to build, test, and improve a disciplined small-cap investing and trading process. The portfolio starts with a maximum paper capital reference of Rs. 5,00,000 and focuses on listed Indian small-cap and micro-cap companies that show the combined evidence of business acceleration, market leadership, sector participation, institutional or peer validation where available, and tradable technical structure.

The portfolio is not a prediction engine and not a stock-tip list. Its primary purpose is to learn how to select stocks, allocate paper capital, control risk, backtest strategies, improve decision quality, and convert Agent Adda research into a repeatable portfolio management process.

The core thesis is that small-cap winners can generate asymmetric returns over a 1-2 year holding horizon when business quality, earnings acceleration, sector tailwinds, market breadth, relative strength, liquidity, and risk controls align. The same segment also carries materially higher volatility, liquidity gaps, governance risk, and drawdown risk, so the portfolio must be run with hard position limits, stop-loss rules, staged entries, and a documented evidence trail before every paper trade.

## 2. Business Case

### 2.1 Why Small Cap

Small-cap investing is attractive because the universe contains companies that can still re-rate from a low institutional base, compound through sector cycles, and respond strongly to earnings upgrades, capex cycles, regulatory changes, operating leverage, and management execution. In many cases, the market recognizes these changes gradually, which creates an opportunity for a disciplined research process to identify leaders before they become obvious index or institutional consensus positions.

The NSE Nifty Smallcap 250 is a useful reference universe because it represents companies ranked 251-500 from the Nifty 500 and is intended to measure the performance of small market capitalisation companies. NSE's index page also records that the index represented about 8.92% of the free-float market capitalization of NSE-listed stocks as of 2026-03-30 and that its constituents contributed about 19.36% of total traded value over the six months ending March 2026. This makes the segment meaningful enough for research, but still narrow enough to require liquidity discipline.

Small-cap funds and indices are also treated as high-risk products in the regulated investment ecosystem. AMFI publishes stress-test and liquidity-analysis disclosures for mid-cap and small-cap mutual funds, and SEBI's investor education material describes the Riskometer framework, including the "Very High Risk" category for volatile and risky products suitable only for aggressive risk profiles. This paper portfolio adopts that risk posture internally even though it is not an external fund.

### 2.2 Why Build A Paper Portfolio Before Live Investing

The business value is not only paper profit. The larger value is building an auditable investment operating system:

- A clear mandate instead of ad hoc stock picking.
- A repeatable scorecard for stock selection.
- Portfolio sizing rules for a Rs. 5,00,000 corpus.
- Risk budgets that prevent one idea from damaging the portfolio.
- Entry, add, trim, exit, and stop-loss discipline.
- Backtesting and walk-forward validation before strategy promotion.
- A journal that captures decision quality, not only outcome.
- A learning loop that improves rules over time from actual evidence.

The paper portfolio gives us a low-cost environment to answer practical questions before any live capital is considered:

- Which small-cap themes are actually producing market leadership?
- Which stocks pass both fundamental and technical quality gates?
- What allocation size is large enough to matter but small enough to survive drawdowns?
- Which stop-loss models work for Indian small-cap volatility?
- When should the portfolio hold cash instead of forcing deployment?
- Which strategy families survive costs, gaps, liquidity filters, and regime changes?
- Does Agent Adda research improve benchmark-relative performance after realistic execution assumptions?

### 2.3 Edge Hypothesis

The portfolio's proposed edge comes from combining five layers that are often used separately:

1. Theme awareness: policy, sector rotation, capex, consumption, credit cycle, export cycle, regulatory changes, and market-flow trends.
2. Fundamental acceleration: sales growth, profit growth, margin expansion, return ratios, balance-sheet strength, cash conversion, and management execution.
3. Technical confirmation: Stage 2 structure, relative strength leadership, price above key moving averages, volume expansion, base breakout, retest, and clear invalidation levels.
4. Portfolio discipline: risk-based sizing, sector caps, cash rules, staged entries, adds only after confirmation, and stop-loss enforcement.
5. Learning system: backtests, paper trades, postmortems, source grading, and rule revisions based on evidence rather than hindsight.

The portfolio should outperform only if the complete process works. A strong story without price confirmation is not enough. A breakout without fundamental quality is not enough. A great stock at the wrong size is still a portfolio mistake.

### 2.4 Success Criteria

The first 90-day paper phase is successful only if the portfolio proves process quality, not merely mark-to-market gains.

Minimum success criteria:

- 30 or more closed paper trades before increasing deployment beyond Phase 1 limits.
- Positive trade expectancy after estimated brokerage and slippage.
- Profit factor above 1.4 across closed trades.
- Maximum drawdown below 10% of corpus during Phase 1.
- No single closed loss above 1% of corpus.
- Total open risk kept at or below 6% of corpus unless explicitly approved by policy.
- Benchmark comparison against Nifty Smallcap 250 TRI, Nifty 500 TRI, and cash.
- Every trade has a pre-entry thesis, scorecard, stop, sizing calculation, and exit reason.
- Every losing trade has a postmortem classified as process error, thesis failure, market regime failure, execution issue, liquidity issue, or acceptable loss.

## 3. Portfolio Mandate

### 3.1 Objective

Build a concentrated but risk-managed internal paper portfolio of Indian small-cap and micro-cap "super performer" candidates with a 1-2 year thesis horizon and a trading framework that can enter, add, trim, and exit based on evidence.

The portfolio aims to maximize risk-adjusted paper returns while developing a repeatable selection and sizing process that can be measured against relevant benchmarks.

### 3.2 Investment Style

The portfolio is a hybrid of:

- Thematic investing.
- Fundamental growth and quality screening.
- Relative-strength momentum.
- Weinstein Stage 2 trend following.
- Breakout, retest, and pullback trading.
- Risk-based portfolio construction.

### 3.3 Instruments

Allowed in MVP:

- NSE/BSE listed cash equities.
- Long-only paper positions.
- No leverage.
- No short selling.
- No options or futures.
- No SME-only names unless a separate liquidity and governance exception is approved.

Future versions may study derivatives only for hedging research, not for MVP execution.

### 3.4 Time Horizon

The portfolio has two horizons:

- Thesis horizon: 1-2 years.
- Trade-management horizon: daily to weekly, depending on technical structure and risk.

This means a company can remain on the research bench for a long time, but paper capital is committed only when the trade setup gives an acceptable entry, stop, and reward/risk.

## 4. Core Principles

1. Risk comes before return.
2. No paper trade without an evidence pack.
3. No averaging down.
4. Cash is an active position when setups are poor.
5. Buy strength with structure, not random weakness.
6. Prefer leaders in improving sectors.
7. Avoid illiquid moves that cannot be entered or exited realistically.
8. Fundamentals decide quality; technicals decide timing.
9. Management, governance, leverage, receivables, inventory, and cash conversion can veto any stock.
10. A theme is useful only when confirmed by price, breadth, earnings, and liquidity.
11. Every position must have a stop before entry.
12. Position size is determined by risk to stop, not excitement about the story.
13. Adds require proof; trims require discipline; exits require obedience.
14. The portfolio must learn from completed trades and update rules only through documented review.
15. The process must remain reproducible from data, scorecards, decisions, and artifacts.

## 5. Universe Policy

### 5.1 Primary Universe

The portfolio may source candidates from:

- Nifty Smallcap 250.
- Nifty Smallcap 500.
- Nifty Microcap 250.
- Local Agent Adda small-cap and micro-cap screens.
- Local `scores.stage_snapshots`, `market.equity_eod`, and fundamental score tables.
- Mutual-fund holdings source bundle for institutional validation.
- Exchange filings, company results, investor presentations, and annual reports.

### 5.2 Market-Cap Bias

Preferred zones:

- Small cap: companies below the large/mid-cap boundary and within the small-cap index ecosystem.
- Micro cap: companies with market cap up to Rs. 5,000 Cr where liquidity and governance are acceptable.
- Adjacent mid-small names may be researched when they have a clear sector leadership role, but they should be tagged separately so the portfolio does not drift away from the mandate.

### 5.3 Minimum Data Requirements

A stock cannot become an active candidate unless the portfolio has:

- Current OHLCV data.
- Market-cap and sector classification.
- Latest available quarterly or annual financial evidence.
- At least one liquidity measure, preferably average traded value.
- Technical stage and relative-strength evidence.
- A clear entry trigger and invalidation level.
- A documented source trail.

### 5.4 Exclusion Rules

Exclude or quarantine stocks with:

- Poor or missing liquidity.
- Repeated lower circuits or abnormal price behavior without evidence.
- Auditor resignation or adverse audit qualification.
- Pledged promoter holdings above policy tolerance without clear mitigation.
- Frequent equity dilution without value creation.
- Weak cash conversion masked by accounting profit.
- Rising debt with weak interest coverage.
- Persistent receivable or inventory stress.
- Unexplained related-party transactions.
- Repeated regulatory or exchange surveillance concerns.
- No reliable financial data.
- No logical stop-loss level within acceptable risk.
- Pure social-media momentum without fundamentals and volume quality.

## 6. Theme Policy

### 6.1 Theme Sources

Themes should come from evidence, not narrative alone. Acceptable theme inputs include:

- Sector rotation and relative-strength reports.
- Breadth and Stage 2 concentration by sector.
- Government policy, budget, infrastructure spending, import/export rules, and regulatory changes.
- Order-book cycles, capex cycles, and industry capacity cycles.
- Quarterly results, guidance, management commentary, and investor presentations.
- FII/DII flow regime and broader risk appetite.
- Mutual-fund holdings and changes where source data is available.
- Peer-group technical and fundamental confirmation.

### 6.2 Initial Theme Buckets

Based on current local Agent Adda small-cap research, initial research buckets are:

- Capital goods, infrastructure, engineering, and capex beneficiaries.
- Healthcare, pharma, diagnostics, and specialty healthcare services.
- Financial services, brokers, exchanges, wealth platforms, and lenders.
- Auto components, EV ancillaries, industrial suppliers, and mobility ecosystem names.
- Chemicals and specialty materials.
- Consumer, retail, hotels, branded products, and premiumization.
- Export or niche manufacturing where earnings acceleration is visible.

These are research buckets, not automatic buy lists. A sector can be thematically attractive while individual stocks remain uninvestable due to valuation, weak setup, poor liquidity, or governance risk.

### 6.3 Theme Lifecycle

Each theme has a lifecycle status:

- `IDEA`: narrative exists, evidence incomplete.
- `BUILDING`: sector breadth or fundamentals improving.
- `CONFIRMED`: price leadership, breadth, and fundamentals align.
- `CROWDED`: extended valuations or stretched technicals.
- `DISTRIBUTION`: leaders fail, breadth weakens, or earnings disappoint.
- `REJECTED`: evidence no longer supports allocation.

New capital is allowed only in `BUILDING` or `CONFIRMED` themes. `CROWDED` themes may allow holds and trims but not fresh entries unless risk/reward is exceptional.

## 7. Stock Selection Scorecard

Every active candidate receives a 100-point score.

| Category | Weight | What It Measures |
|---|---:|---|
| Theme fit | 15 | Sector tailwind, policy/cycle support, peer confirmation, fund-flow relevance |
| Fundamental acceleration and quality | 25 | Sales/profit growth, margins, ROCE/ROE, leverage, cash conversion, balance-sheet quality |
| Technical setup | 25 | Stage 2, RS leadership, moving-average structure, breakout/retest quality, volume behavior |
| Liquidity and tradeability | 15 | Average traded value, spread behavior, circuit risk, entry/exit practicality |
| Governance and management | 10 | Promoter behavior, disclosures, pledge, audit quality, capital allocation, execution history |
| Valuation and reward/risk | 10 | Valuation versus growth, downside to stop, upside runway, peer comparison |

### 7.1 Candidate Ratings

| Score | Rating | Action |
|---:|---|---|
| 75+ | Core Candidate | Eligible for active watch and paper entry when trigger appears |
| 65-74 | Active Watch | Needs trigger or one missing evidence layer |
| 55-64 | Research Watch | Keep tracking; not eligible for capital |
| Below 55 | Reject or Monitor | No paper allocation |

### 7.2 Non-Negotiable Gates

A stock cannot be bought even with a high score unless all gates pass:

- Liquidity gate passes.
- No critical governance veto.
- Latest financial evidence is not stale beyond policy tolerance.
- Stop-loss distance is acceptable.
- Minimum reward/risk is 2:1 after costs.
- Position sizing fits corpus, sector cap, and open-risk cap.
- Stock is not too extended from the intended entry zone.

## 8. Portfolio Construction Policy

### 8.1 Corpus

Starting paper capital is Rs. 5,00,000. All sizing percentages are calculated on current NAV, not original corpus, after the first daily mark-to-market.

### 8.2 Target Structure

| Item | Policy |
|---|---:|
| Target number of open positions | 6-10 |
| Maximum open positions | 12 |
| Initial position size | 3-4% of NAV |
| Normal full position size | 7-8% of NAV |
| Hard single-stock cap | 10% of NAV |
| Hard sector cap | 25% of NAV |
| Risk per new trade | 0.75% of NAV |
| High-conviction risk exception | 1.00% of NAV |
| Total open risk cap | 6% of NAV |
| Absolute stress risk cap | 8% of NAV |
| Maximum gross exposure after Phase 3 | 95% of NAV |

### 8.3 Rupee Limits At Rs. 5,00,000 NAV

| Rule | Rupee Value |
|---|---:|
| Initial position, 3% | Rs. 15,000 |
| Initial position, 4% | Rs. 20,000 |
| Full position, 7% | Rs. 35,000 |
| Full position, 8% | Rs. 40,000 |
| Hard single-stock cap, 10% | Rs. 50,000 |
| Sector cap, 25% | Rs. 1,25,000 |
| Risk per trade, 0.75% | Rs. 3,750 |
| High-conviction risk, 1.00% | Rs. 5,000 |
| Total open risk cap, 6% | Rs. 30,000 |
| Stress risk cap, 8% | Rs. 40,000 |

### 8.4 Position Sizing Formula

The paper quantity is the minimum of four constraints:

```text
risk_quantity      = floor(allowed_trade_risk / abs(entry_price - stop_price))
position_quantity  = floor(max_position_value / entry_price)
cash_quantity      = floor(available_cash / entry_price)
liquidity_quantity = floor(max_daily_participation_value / entry_price)

final_quantity = min(risk_quantity, position_quantity, cash_quantity, liquidity_quantity)
```

The order is rejected if `final_quantity <= 0`.

For small-cap stocks, liquidity participation must be conservative. The paper portfolio should not assume it can buy or sell more than a small share of average daily traded value. The default cap is 2% of 20-day average traded value for entry and 1% for exit stress simulation unless a stricter stock-specific rule is set.

### 8.5 Deployment Phases

The portfolio does not deploy 95% exposure on day one. Exposure is earned through evidence.

| Phase | Exposure Limit | Promotion Requirement |
|---|---:|---|
| Phase 0 - Research | 0% | Policy approved, watchlist built, scorecards ready |
| Phase 1 - Pilot | 40% | First paper trades only; validate sizing, stops, journal |
| Phase 2 - Expansion | 70% | 30+ closed trades, positive expectancy, drawdown within limits |
| Phase 3 - Full Paper Portfolio | 95% | Robust backtest plus stable live-paper process |

The portfolio can always move down a phase after drawdown, data-quality failure, or market regime deterioration.

### 8.6 Cash Policy

Cash is not failure. Cash is required when:

- Index breadth is weak.
- Small-cap leadership is narrow or extended.
- Valid candidates fail entry rules.
- Stops are too wide.
- Earnings season creates avoidable gap risk.
- Liquidity deteriorates.
- The portfolio open-risk cap is full.

## 9. Entry Policy

### 9.1 Valid Entry Types

Allowed entry setups:

- Stage 2 base breakout with volume expansion.
- Breakout retest and hold above prior resistance.
- Pullback to rising 20DMA, 50DMA, or equivalent support inside Stage 2.
- Stage 1-to-Stage 2 transition with improving RS and volume.
- Earnings breakout after confirmed results and clean management commentary.
- Sector-leader continuation when sector breadth is improving.

### 9.2 Entry Confirmation

A paper buy requires:

- Candidate rating of `Core Candidate` or `Active Watch`.
- Theme status of `BUILDING` or `CONFIRMED`.
- Price trigger defined before the session.
- Stop defined before the order.
- Reward/risk of at least 2:1.
- Volume confirmation or retest confirmation.
- Relative strength improving versus benchmark and sector.
- No immediate earnings or event gap risk unless explicitly accepted.
- Liquidity pass.
- Portfolio risk pass.

### 9.3 No-Chase Rule

If price is more than 5% above the planned trigger before execution, the order is skipped or converted to a retest watch. A strong stock can be bought later; a poor entry damages expectancy.

### 9.4 Paper Execution Assumption

Default paper execution is next-session market-on-open or a pre-defined limit order after the trigger is confirmed. Intraday chasing is not part of MVP.

## 10. Add, Trim, And Exit Policy

### 10.1 Adds

Adding is allowed only when the stock proves the thesis.

Rules:

- No averaging down.
- First add only after the position is up at least +1R or forms a valid retest/new-high setup.
- Add size must be smaller than or equal to the initial position.
- Add risk budget is 0.35-0.50% of NAV.
- No add if sector cap, position cap, or open-risk cap is breached.
- No add if price is extended more than 12% above the 50DMA unless the stop can be trailed without increasing risk.
- No add ahead of high-gap-risk events without explicit approval.

### 10.2 Trims

Trim when:

- Position exceeds 10% of NAV.
- Position becomes technically extended after a sharp move.
- A 3R or higher move is reached and the stock shows exhaustion.
- Sector exposure exceeds 25%.
- Open risk exceeds policy limits.
- Earnings or news event increases gap risk.
- The position remains profitable but relative strength starts deteriorating.

Default trim action is to reduce the position toward 7-8% of NAV unless the exit rule requires a full sell.

### 10.3 Exits

Exit when:

- Stop-loss is hit or breached on close, depending on stop type.
- Stock moves from Stage 2 into Stage 3/4 deterioration.
- Price closes below the 50DMA with distribution volume and no immediate recovery.
- Relative strength breaks below policy threshold.
- Fundamental thesis breaks.
- Management/governance red flag appears.
- Sector thesis invalidates.
- Liquidity collapses.
- Better opportunities require capital and the current stock no longer meets rating threshold.

## 11. Stop-Loss Policy

### 11.1 Stop Types

Allowed stop methods:

- Structure stop below breakout base or retest low.
- ATR stop, default 2 ATR below entry for volatile names.
- Moving-average stop below rising 50DMA for trend holds.
- Time stop if a breakout fails to progress after a defined period.
- Thesis stop after fundamental or governance deterioration.

### 11.2 Stop Distance

Preferred initial stop distance is 8-10%. Hard maximum initial stop distance is 12% for normal trades. If the logical stop is wider than 12%, the portfolio must reduce size or skip the trade.

The policy favors smaller position size over arbitrary tight stops. A stop should be placed where the setup is invalidated, not where the desired position size becomes convenient.

### 11.3 Stop Discipline

Stop-loss breaches are not debated after the fact. The only allowed exception is a pre-defined close-based stop where the policy explicitly waits for closing confirmation. Even then, if gap or liquidity risk becomes abnormal, the risk manager can force exit.

## 12. Risk Assessment Framework

### 12.1 Risk Types

The portfolio tracks:

- Market risk: broad small-cap drawdown and volatility.
- Sector risk: too many stocks tied to the same cycle.
- Stock-specific risk: earnings miss, governance, debt, management failure.
- Liquidity risk: inability to exit near modeled price.
- Gap risk: overnight results, news, policy, or surveillance moves.
- Valuation risk: high expectations with limited margin of safety.
- Data risk: stale or incorrect fundamentals, prices, or symbol mapping.
- Model risk: overfitted backtests and unstable rules.
- Behavioral risk: chasing, averaging down, ignoring stops, changing rules during drawdown.

### 12.2 Portfolio Risk Dashboard

Daily dashboard must show:

- NAV and cash.
- Gross exposure.
- Open positions.
- Sector exposure.
- Stock-level weight.
- Stop level for every position.
- Rupee risk to stop for every position.
- Total open risk.
- Biggest gap-risk names.
- Liquidity flags.
- Pending entries, adds, trims, and exits.
- Benchmark comparison.

### 12.3 Drawdown Actions

| Drawdown From Peak | Required Action |
|---:|---|
| 3% | Review new entries; no change required if process clean |
| 5% | Freeze adds; only highest-quality new entries allowed |
| 7% | Reduce exposure, tighten review, stop Phase promotion |
| 10% | Move to capital-protection mode; no new trades |
| 12% | Full policy review before any new risk |

## 13. Backtesting And Validation Policy

### 13.1 Required Tests

Before any strategy is promoted from research to active paper use, it must be tested through Portfolio Lab or equivalent deterministic replay.

Minimum report:

- Test period.
- Data snapshot.
- Universe definition.
- Entry and exit rules.
- Position-sizing rules.
- Stop-loss rules.
- Cost and slippage assumptions.
- Number of trades.
- Closed trade count.
- Hit rate.
- Average win and average loss.
- Expectancy.
- Profit factor.
- Maximum drawdown.
- Exposure.
- Turnover.
- Benchmark return.
- Benchmark excess return.
- Sector attribution.
- Top winners and losers.
- Failure modes.
- Data-quality exclusions.

### 13.2 Benchmark Set

Compare against:

- Nifty Smallcap 250 TRI.
- Nifty 500 TRI.
- Equal-weight selected universe.
- Cash.

### 13.3 Overfitting Controls

Rules:

- No strategy promotion based on one short lucky period.
- Prefer simple rules over complex parameter tuning.
- Use train, validation, and locked test periods where data allows.
- Record rejected strategies and why they failed.
- Check liquidity and gap assumptions separately.
- Do not change a live-paper rule to rescue an open trade.

## 14. Learning And Intelligence Loop

The portfolio is expected to become more market-aware over time, but learning must be controlled. The system may recommend rule changes only from documented evidence.

### 14.1 Trade Journal Fields

Every trade record must include:

- Date.
- Symbol.
- Sector.
- Theme.
- Candidate score.
- Rating.
- Entry setup.
- Entry price.
- Stop price.
- Target or expected reward/risk.
- Position size.
- Risk amount.
- Evidence links.
- Reason for entry.
- Reason for skip, if skipped.
- Add/trim/exit decisions.
- Final P&L.
- R multiple.
- Exit reason.
- Postmortem classification.
- Lesson learned.

### 14.2 Weekly Review

Weekly review must answer:

- Which themes gained or lost leadership?
- Which candidates moved from research to active watch?
- Which positions violated thesis, technicals, or risk?
- Which trades followed policy?
- Which trades broke policy?
- Is cash too high because of discipline or missed process?
- Are stops too tight, too loose, or effective?
- Did the portfolio outperform the relevant benchmark for the right reasons?

### 14.3 Monthly Policy Review

Monthly policy review may adjust:

- Candidate scoring weights.
- Liquidity thresholds.
- Stop method by volatility regime.
- Sector caps.
- Deployment phase.
- Strategy inclusion or exclusion.

Policy changes require written rationale and should not be made merely to justify an existing losing trade.

## 16. Governance

### 16.1 Role Separation

Research engine:

- Finds themes.
- Builds candidate lists.
- Collects financial, technical, and source evidence.
- Produces scorecards.

Strategy engine:

- Converts eligible candidates into rules.
- Backtests entries, exits, stops, and costs.
- Reports metrics.

Portfolio manager:

- Allocates capital.
- Enforces risk.
- Approves, skips, trims, or exits paper orders.

Reviewer:

- Challenges assumptions.
- Reviews postmortems.
- Approves policy changes.

### 16.2 Decision Rights

Only deterministic policy rules can create final paper orders. LLM or narrative research can recommend, challenge, and explain, but cannot bypass sizing, stop, liquidity, or risk checks.

### 16.3 Change Control

Policy changes must record:

- Date.
- Rule changed.
- Reason.
- Evidence.
- Expected impact.
- Backtest or paper evidence.
- Approval status.

During drawdown, changes that increase risk are prohibited unless the policy review explicitly approves them after evidence review.

## 19. Policy Statement

The Smallcap Super Performers Portfolio will operate as a research-first, risk-first, paper-only model portfolio governed by fund-style discipline. It will seek asymmetric small-cap opportunities, but it will not compromise on liquidity, governance, stop-loss discipline, position sizing, or source evidence. The portfolio's first job is to build a process that survives real market behavior. Paper returns are useful only when they come from repeatable decisions, controlled risk, and documented learning.

Research only. Not investment advice. Not a recommendation to buy, sell, or hold any security.
