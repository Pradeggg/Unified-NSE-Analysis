## Executive Summary

This note details the proposed trading and portfolio-management strategy for the Agent Adda SmallCap and MidCap paper/model portfolios with total deployed capital capped at Rs. 3,00,000.

The strategy is research-first and paper-only. It does not approve live investment, external solicitation, pooled capital, PMS, AIF, mutual-fund activity, or regulated advisory activity. The purpose is to test whether Agent Adda can convert research, scoring, sector context, technical structure, and risk controls into a repeatable portfolio process.

The recommended allocation is:

| Sleeve | Allocation | Amount | Role |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| SmallCap | 45% | Rs. 1,35,000 | Higher-alpha sleeve with stricter governance, liquidity, and event-risk checks |
| MidCap | 55% | Rs. 1,65,000 | More liquid leadership sleeve with stronger stability and institutional-following bias |
| Combined | 100% | Rs. 3,00,000 | Total paper/model deployed-capital cap |

The combined portfolio should not force full deployment. Capital should be committed only when a stock clears evidence, trigger, stop-loss, liquidity, and governance gates.

## 1. Portfolio Objective

The objective is to build a disciplined paper-trading process that can answer three questions:

1. Can Agent Adda identify quality SmallCap and MidCap leaders before they become obvious consensus names?
2. Can the process enter only when fundamentals, sector strength, price action, liquidity, and governance evidence align?
3. Can risk be controlled so that one weak stock, failed result, or sector reversal does not damage the overall portfolio?

The portfolio is not a stock-tip list. A stock can remain on the watchlist and still be blocked from deployment.

## 2. Strategy Philosophy

The strategy follows four principles:

1. Fundamentals decide quality.
2. Technicals decide timing.
3. Portfolio risk decides size.
4. Governance and liquidity can veto any trade.

The preferred setup is long-only cash equity. The MVP strategy excludes leverage, short selling, futures, options, and SME-only names unless a separate liquidity and governance exception is approved.

### 3.2 Why 45% SmallCap And 55% MidCap

SmallCap has higher upside but higher risk:

- More volatile price action.
- Higher gap risk around results and corporate actions.
- Lower liquidity in weaker market conditions.
- Greater governance and disclosure risk.
- Higher probability of sharp drawdowns after extended moves.

MidCap gets a slightly higher allocation because:

- Liquidity is generally better.
- Institutional participation is usually stronger.
- Price discovery is more stable than in smallcaps.
- Portfolio drawdown control is easier.
- Result and filing evidence is usually easier to verify.

The split is intended to preserve upside while avoiding a portfolio that is overexposed to smallcap event risk.

## 4. SmallCap Sleeve Strategy

### 4.1 Mandate

The SmallCap sleeve focuses on Indian small-cap and micro-cap listed equities where the following align:

- Business acceleration.
- Earnings growth.
- Sales growth.
- Sector leadership.
- Stage 2 technical structure.
- Improving relative strength.
- Acceptable liquidity.
- Clean governance.
- Clear entry trigger and stop-loss.

Reference universes include Nifty Smallcap 250, Nifty Smallcap 500, Nifty Microcap 250, Agent Adda local smallcap screens, local stage snapshots, local EOD market data, exchange filings, company results, investor presentations, annual reports, and mutual-fund holding evidence where available.

### 4.2 SmallCap Scorecard

Each active candidate should be scored out of 100:

| Category | Weight | What It Measures |
|---|---:|---|
| Theme fit | 15 | Sector tailwind, cycle support, peer confirmation, fund-flow relevance |
| Fundamental acceleration and quality | 25 | Sales growth, profit growth, margins, ROCE/ROE, leverage, cash conversion |
| Technical setup | 25 | Stage 2, relative strength, moving-average structure, breakout/retest quality |
| Liquidity and tradeability | 15 | Average traded value, spread behavior, circuit risk, exit practicality |
| Governance and management | 10 | Promoter behavior, pledge, audit quality, disclosures, capital allocation |
| Valuation and reward/risk | 10 | Valuation versus growth, stop distance, upside runway, peer comparison |

Candidate rating:

| Score | Rating | Action |
|---:|---|---|
| 75+ | Core Candidate | Eligible for active watch and paper entry when trigger appears |
| 65-74 | Active Watch | Needs trigger or one missing evidence layer |
| 55-64 | Research Watch | Track only; not eligible for capital |
| Below 55 | Reject or Monitor | No paper allocation |

### 4.3 SmallCap Entry Requirements

A SmallCap paper entry requires all of the following:

- Core Candidate or Active Watch rating.
- Theme status is Building or Confirmed.
- Fresh quarterly or annual financial evidence.
- No unresolved governance blocker.
- Price trigger defined before the session.
- Stop-loss defined before entry.
- Reward/risk at least 2:1.
- Volume confirmation or retest confirmation.
- Relative strength improving versus benchmark and sector.
- Liquidity pass.
- Portfolio risk pass.

### 4.4 SmallCap No-Chase Rule

If price is more than 5% above the planned trigger before execution, the order should be skipped or moved to retest watch.

The strategy must prefer a missed trade over a poor entry. A strong stock can be reviewed later, but a bad entry damages expectancy.

### 5.2 Required MidCap Gates

- Stage 2 or clear Stage 2 proxy.
- Growth score above threshold.
- High EPS or earnings-quality proxy above threshold.
- YoY sales-growth score above threshold.
- Sector theme aligned with market leadership.
- Government-investment or structural demand alignment where relevant.
- Liquidity sufficient for paper execution.
- No-chase rule when RSI or price extension is excessive.

### 5.3 MidCap Decision Buckets

## 6. Risk Framework On Rs. 3,00,000

### 6.1 Risk Limits

| Risk Rule | Limit |
|---|---:|
| Normal risk per new trade | Rs. 1,500-2,250 |
| High-conviction max risk per trade | Rs. 3,000 |
| Total open risk cap | Rs. 18,000 |
| Sector cap | Rs. 75,000 |
| Single-stock cap | Rs. 30,000 |
| Minimum reward/risk | 2:1 |
| Averaging down | Not allowed |
| Chasing above trigger | Not allowed |

### 6.2 Position Sizing Logic

Position size should be determined by stop-loss risk, not by excitement about the stock.

```text
risk_quantity      = floor(allowed_trade_risk / abs(entry_price - stop_price))
position_quantity  = floor(max_position_value / entry_price)
cash_quantity      = floor(available_cash / entry_price)
liquidity_quantity = floor(max_daily_participation_value / entry_price)

final_quantity = min(risk_quantity, position_quantity, cash_quantity, liquidity_quantity)
```

The order is rejected if final quantity is zero or if the position breaches single-stock, sector, cash, or liquidity rules.

### 6.3 Stop-Distance Examples

| Stop Distance | Position Value At Rs. 1,500 Risk | Position Value At Rs. 2,250 Risk | Position Value At Rs. 3,000 Risk |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 8% | Rs. 18,750 | Rs. 28,125 | Rs. 37,500 |
| 10% | Rs. 15,000 | Rs. 22,500 | Rs. 30,000 |
| 12% | Rs. 12,500 | Rs. 18,750 | Rs. 25,000 |

Because the single-stock cap is Rs. 30,000, even a high-conviction trade should not exceed Rs. 30,000 without explicit review.

If the logical stop is wider than 12%, the portfolio should reduce size or skip the trade. The stop should be placed where the setup is invalidated, not where the desired position size becomes convenient.

## 7. Entry, Add, Trim, And Exit Rules

### 7.1 Entry Rules

A paper entry is allowed only when:

- Candidate rating is Core Candidate or Active Watch.
- Technical setup is Stage 2 or accepted Stage 2 proxy.
- Price confirms breakout, retest hold, or structured pullback.
- Volume confirms the move.
- Relative strength is improving.
- Latest result/filing is checked.
- Governance review is complete.
- Stop-loss is defined before entry.
- Reward/risk is at least 2:1.
- Position size fits the Rs. 3,00,000 risk framework.

### 7.2 Add Rules

Adding is allowed only after proof:

- No averaging down.
- First add only after +1R or a valid retest/new-high setup.
- Add size should be equal to or smaller than the initial position.
- Add risk budget should be smaller than initial risk.
- No add if sector cap, single-stock cap, or total open-risk cap is breached.
- No add before high-gap-risk result or corporate event unless explicitly reviewed.

### 7.3 Trim Rules

Trim when:

- A position approaches or exceeds Rs. 30,000.
- Sector exposure approaches or exceeds Rs. 75,000.
- Position becomes technically extended.
- Price reaches 3R and shows exhaustion.
- Relative strength starts deteriorating.
- Earnings or news event increases gap risk.
- Open risk exceeds the portfolio cap.

### 7.4 Exit Rules

Exit when:

- Stop-loss is hit.
- Stock moves from Stage 2 into Stage 3 or Stage 4 deterioration.
- Price breaks below a key moving average with distribution volume.
- Relative strength breaks policy threshold.
- Fundamental thesis breaks.
- Governance red flag appears.
- Sector thesis invalidates.
- Liquidity deteriorates.
- Better opportunities require capital and the current stock no longer qualifies.

## 8. Preferred Execution Overlay

The recommended execution overlay is:

```text
Darvas breakout or retest confirmation
+ trend alignment
+ high relative strength
+ official result freshness
+ governance clearance
+ risk-cap fit
```

The funnel should work as follows:

1. Start with the researched universe.
2. Filter for Stage 2 or accepted Stage 2 proxy.
3. Prioritize Darvas breakout or breakout-retest structures.
4. Require Supertrend or trend alignment.
5. Prefer relative-strength leaders.
6. Block any name with stale results, unresolved governance, weak liquidity, or excessive extension.
7. Size only from stop-loss risk.

This means a fundamentally good stock is not enough. A high score is not enough. A price breakout is not enough. The strategy approves review only when all layers align.
